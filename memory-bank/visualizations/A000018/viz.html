OEIS · A000018

Counting without double-counting

? Problem

Some integers are x² + 16y² for whole numbers x, y ≥ 0 — like 16, 17, 20. How many are there up to a bound?

The counts up to 2ⁿ, n = 0..9, are growing — but why can't you just count the (x,y) pairs?

↓

1 Same number, two recipes

1a

Every (x,y) from 0 to 4. Two different cells land on the same number, 16 — linked below.

Counting cells would overcount. The question is how many distinct numbers appear, not how many pairs exist — finding collisions like this one is the real work.

↓

2 A hidden sum of two squares

2a

x² + 16y² is exactly x² + (4y)² — so every number here is automatically a sum of two squares. For 17: 1² + 4² = 17.

That's necessary, not sufficient — sums like 2 = 1²+1² aren't this stricter form, since Fermat's theorem allows any even square, not only a multiple of 16.
