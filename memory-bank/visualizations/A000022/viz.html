OEIS · A000022

One center or two

? Problem

Two carbon skeletons, both 7 atoms, every atom bonded to at most 4 others. A000022 calls one of these centered and counts it; the other it doesn't. What's the difference?

↓

1 Peel away the outside, layer by layer

1a

Left tree: strip every current leaf, then strip the new leaves that exposes, and stop the moment 1 or 2 atoms are left.

1b

Right tree: the exact same peeling rule, applied to a different starting shape.

↓

2 Only two ways it can end

Jordan's theorem (1869): peeling a tree this way always stops at exactly one atom, or exactly two atoms still bonded to each other — never zero, never three or more, never two that aren't bonded. The left tree above is centered; the right is bicentered.

↓

3 Every skeleton is one or the other

3a

Since a tree is never both and never neither, splitting all 7-atom skeletons by outcome accounts for every one of them:

centered (A000022) + bicentered (A000200) = every quartic tree (A000602) — checked against all three sequences' own published terms, not just this one page's count.

↓

✓ Solution

Counting only single-centered skeletons, up through 20 atoms:

✓ verified

peel every leaf → peel what that exposes → stop at one atom or two bonded atoms → keep only the ones that stopped at one
