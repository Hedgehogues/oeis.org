# A000025 — coefficients of Ramanujan's mock theta function f(q)

## Vision
`a(n)` is the `n`-th coefficient of Ramanujan's third-order mock theta function
`f(q) = 1 + Σ_{k≥1} q^(k²)/∏_{i=1}^k (1+qⁱ)²`, one of the functions he defined in his last letter to
Hardy — an object that sits near modular forms but is not itself one. The naive question ("what
does this exotic series produce?") gives no handle on why the numbers behave the way they do. The
page's central claim: `a(n)` equals (number of partitions of `n` with even Dyson rank) minus
(number with odd rank), where rank = largest part minus number of parts — an ordinary,
fully-enumerable partition statistic sits behind an intimidating analytic object, and that bridge
is checked by an independent construction, not merely asserted.

## Architecture

### Overview (prose)
`memory-bank/visualizations/A000025/viz.html` runs Problem (the defining series and the signed
integers it produces, "what are these counting?") → (1: a partition's rank, defined and worked on
one example) → (2: every partition of `n=6` listed and bordered by rank parity, via
[device::RankSignedTally](../../_terms.md#deviceranksignedtally)) → (3: the two colour counts
summed with sign, checked directly against the series' own coefficient of `q⁶`) → Solution (the
bridge stated plainly, with a verified-match badge citing `proof.mjs`'s actual run).

### Code
`sequences/A000025/solution.mjs` computes `a(n)` by expanding the defining `q`-series exactly in
`BigInt` — for each `k` with `k²≤N`, building `∏(1+qⁱ)²` as a truncated polynomial, inverting it as
a formal power series, and accumulating the shifted result. `sequences/A000025/proof.mjs` does not
reuse any of that: it enumerates every actual partition of `n` by recursive descent (no generating
function, no polynomial arithmetic), computes each one's Dyson rank, and sums `+1`/`-1` by parity —
a construction with no shared code, formula, or intermediate representation with the series side —
then compares the two results and, separately, both against OEIS's published terms.
