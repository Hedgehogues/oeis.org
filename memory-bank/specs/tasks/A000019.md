# A000019 — primitive permutation groups of degree n

## Vision
`a(n)` counts the primitive permutation groups of degree `n`, up to conjugacy in `S_n`: groups
acting transitively on `n` points that admit no nontrivial "block system" — no way to split the
points into smaller, equal-sized chunks the group only ever shuffles as whole chunks. The page's
central claim: a block size must divide the point count, so when `n` is prime there is no candidate
size at all and the rotation group of degree `n` is automatically primitive; when `n` is composite,
whether a candidate size actually produces a genuine block has to be checked by trying to build one
and seeing whether the construction stays small or is forced to swallow everything.

## Terms
MUST/MUST NOT/SHALL/SHOULD/SHOULD NOT/MAY as in RFC 2119. Uses two catalogued devices, one new and
one reused unchanged: [device::DivisorChips](../../_terms.md#devicedivisorchips),
[device::BlockClosureTrace](../../_terms.md#deviceblockclosuretrace).

## Scope
**In scope:** `memory-bank/visualizations/A000019/viz.html` — the current, only page for this
sequence (no kept drafts) — and the `solution.mjs`/`proof.mjs` pair that computes and checks the
sequence itself, for the honest range `n=1..6` this exact-enumeration method reaches.

**Out of scope:** the classification of primitive groups beyond degree 6 (which comes from
decades of computational group theory and the classification of finite simple groups, not from a
loop like this one — the same honest limit `sequences/A000001/README.md` states for group counting
past `n=8`); naming every one of the primitive groups this page counts (the picture explains WHY
prime degree forces primitivity, not a full classification of every group found).

## Architecture

### Overview (prose)
`memory-bank/visualizations/A000019/viz.html` runs Problem (two rotation groups, degree 4 and
degree 5, asking which one can be split into smaller equal chunks) → (1: a candidate chunk size
must divide the point count, via
[device::DivisorChips](../../_terms.md#devicedivisorchips) — degree 5's primality leaves no
candidate at all) → (2: trying to actually build a chunk, via
[device::BlockClosureTrace](../../_terms.md#deviceblockclosuretrace) — degree 4's closure stops at
a genuine size-2 chunk, degree 5's is forced to grow, step by step, all the way to everything) →
Solution (the count of 5 primitive groups of degree 5, with an explicit, honest note that primality
only explains the rotation group's own primitivity — the other four groups in that count are
checked the same way but are not individually drawn).

### Code
`sequences/A000019/solution.mjs` computes `a(n)` by exactly the procedure the page draws: an exact
"one-step extension" closure enumerates every subgroup of `S_n` (not an approximation over a bounded
number of generators — a prior draft's shortcut of that kind happened to give correct small-`n`
answers without being provably complete, and was replaced), filters to the transitive ones with no
nontrivial minimal block (the classical union-find closure), and reduces to conjugacy classes.
`sequences/A000019/proof.mjs` does not reuse the block-based primitivity test: it independently
re-confirms every returned class via a different theorem (a point stabilizer is a MAXIMAL subgroup
of a primitive group — Dixon & Mortimer, *Permutation Groups*, Thm. 1.10), re-checks distinctness
with a freshly written conjugacy test, and — because the expensive step is enumerating ALL of
`S_n`'s subgroups, not just the primitive ones — cross-checks that enumeration's completeness
against `OEIS A005432` ("number of subgroups of `S_n`"), a published sequence about a different
question entirely, before comparing final counts to `A000019`'s own published terms.
