# A000015 — smallest prime power ≥ n

## Vision
`a(n)` is the smallest prime power at least `n` (this entry's own convention counts `1` as a prime
power). The per-term rule is a one-line scan. What the page actually demonstrates: because the
sequence is non-decreasing, it decomposes into runs of a repeated value, and a run's length is
exactly the gap between that prime power and the previous one — a genuine, independently checkable
structural fact, not a restatement, even though the per-term rule itself carries no further story.
A candidate stronger claim (that prime powers are meaningfully denser than primes, so their gaps
stay smaller) was investigated and dropped: checked numerically up to 100,000, the maximum gap among
prime powers and among primes alone was identical, since prime squares and cubes are too sparse
below that range to visibly close any gap. The page keeps only the claim it can show.

## Terms
MUST/MUST NOT/SHALL/SHOULD/SHOULD NOT/MAY as in RFC 2119. Uses one catalogued device, reused
unchanged: [device::RunLengthEncoding](../../_terms.md#devicerunlengthencoding).

## Scope
**In scope:** `memory-bank/visualizations/A000015/viz.html` — the current, only page for this
sequence (no kept drafts) — and the `solution.mjs`/`proof.mjs` pair.

**Out of scope:** any claim about the relative density of prime powers versus primes (investigated,
found not to hold in any visually demonstrable range, and deliberately not drawn); the general
theory of prime gaps.

## Architecture

### Overview (prose)
`memory-bank/visualizations/A000015/viz.html` runs Problem (the first 20 terms, `16` appearing three
times, others once) → (1: grouping the sequence into runs, via
[device::RunLengthEncoding](../../_terms.md#devicerunlengthencoding), with the worked example that
run `16`'s length of 3 is the gap since the previous prime power, `13`) → Solution (every run's
length is a checkable gap between two consecutive prime powers).

### Code
`sequences/A000015/solution.mjs` scans upward from `n`, testing each candidate for being a prime
power via trial division to find its smallest prime factor. `sequences/A000015/proof.mjs` does not
reuse that scan: it sieves every `p^k` up to a bound directly (marking, not testing candidates one
at a time), reconstructs the sequence from that sieve, and separately checks the sequence's own
run/gap structure — that every run's length in `a(1)..a(N)` equals the gap to the previous prime
power, with the one exception of the final, possibly-truncated run — before comparing final values
to OEIS's published terms.
