# A000009 — partitions of n into distinct parts

## Vision
`a(n)` counts the partitions of `n` into parts each used at most once. The naive question ("how
many distinct-part partitions?") gives no hint that this count is secretly identical to a
differently-restricted count: partitions of `n` into odd parts only. The page's central claim is
Euler's theorem itself (the `d=2` case of the more general Glaisher's theorem): every odd-parts
partition maps, by one explicit rule (group equal parts, write each group's multiplicity in binary,
expand into powers-of-two multiples of the odd value), to exactly one distinct-parts partition, and
this map is a bijection — witnessed here concretely on all of `n=8`'s partitions, not merely
asserted from two equal counts.

## Architecture

### Overview (prose)
`memory-bank/visualizations/A000009/viz.html` runs Problem (partitions of 8 into distinct parts
versus into odd parts, "how many of each?") → (1: both full lists written out, 6 and 6) → (2: the
matching rule stated once, with one worked example — three copies of 1, multiplicity 3 in binary,
splitting into parts 1 and 2) → (3: the rule applied to every one of the 6 odd-parts partitions,
via [device::PartitionBijectionMatch](../../_terms.md#devicepartitionbijectionmatch), landing
exactly on the 6 distinct-parts partitions) → Solution (the count, Euler's theorem named explicitly
as the `d=2` case of Glaisher's theorem, and a verified-match badge citing `proof.mjs`'s actual
run).

### Code
`sequences/A000009/solution.mjs` computes `a(n)` by the direct 0/1-knapsack recurrence over part
sizes `1..n` (each part used at most once) — distinct parts, read from the definition, in exact
`BigInt` arithmetic. `sequences/A000009/proof.mjs` does not reuse that recurrence: it independently
implements an UNBOUNDED knapsack over odd part sizes only (Euler's theorem's other side), checks
agreement between the two structurally different constructions for every `n` tested, then
separately enumerates the actual partitions of one concrete `n` and applies Euler's explicit
binary-expansion map to every odd-parts partition, checking the result is exactly the set of
distinct-parts partitions with no omissions and no collisions, before comparing final counts to
OEIS's published terms.
