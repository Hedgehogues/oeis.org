# A000023 — permutations signed by fixed-point parity

## Vision
`a(n)` is the number of permutations of `{1,...,n}` with an even number of fixed points minus the
number with an odd number of fixed points. The naive question ("count and subtract") gives no
handle on why the total lands where it does. The page's central claim: bucketing permutations by
their exact fixed-point count `k` turns the whole signed count into a visible inclusion–exclusion
sum — `C(n,k)` ways to choose the fixed points times `D(n-k)` derangements of the rest, signed by
the parity of `k` — and a second, independent route (a simple linear recurrence falling out of the
sequence's own exponential generating function) reaches the identical number without any buckets.

## Architecture

### Overview (prose)
`memory-bank/visualizations/A000023/viz.html` runs Problem (24 permutations of 4 elements, scored
`+1`/`-1` by fixed-point parity) → (1: via
[device::SignedBucketSum](../../_terms.md#devicesignedbucketsum), all 5 fixed-point-count buckets
for `n=4`, each a `C(4,k)·D(4-k)` count, signed by the parity of `k`) → (2: the signed buckets
summed to `8`) → (3: the identical `8` reached again by the recurrence `a(n)=n·a(n-1)+(-2)^n`, no
buckets involved) → Solution (the same signed count charted for `n=0..12`, magnitude on a log
scale, colour carrying the sign, a verified-match badge).

### Code
`sequences/A000023/solution.mjs` computes `a(n)` by the plain definition: generate every
permutation of `n` elements (recursive swap-based generation), count its fixed points, add `+1` or
`-1` by parity. `sequences/A000023/proof.mjs` does not reuse that search: it independently computes
derangement counts from their own two-term recurrence and sums `C(n,k)·D(n-k)·(-1)^k` over `k`
(the inclusion–exclusion route), and separately computes the linear recurrence
`a(n)=n·a(n-1)+(-2)^n` — two structurally different, from-scratch routes — before comparing all
three (brute force, bucket sum, recurrence) to each other and to OEIS's published terms.
