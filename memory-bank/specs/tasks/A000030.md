# A000030 — initial digit of n

## Vision
`a(n)` is the leading digit of `n`. The per-term rule is a one-line restatement of positional
notation — no explanatory picture is owed to it, and OEIS's own entry lists the sequence's
self-similar block structure as an "Equivalent definition," confirming it is a restatement rather
than a finding. The page's actual subject is a statistical question ABOUT this sequence: does the
proportion of numbers leading with each digit settle down as you count further? The page's central
claim: for A000030 itself, no — the natural-density proportion oscillates forever between roughly
11% and 56% — while the identical digit-extraction rule applied to powers of two (`A008952`) settles
onto Benford's law and stays there, because the fractional parts of `n·log₁₀2` are equidistributed
(`log₁₀2` irrational) while the fractional parts of `log₁₀n` are skewed. The contrast, not either
sequence alone, is the explanatory content.

## Terms
MUST/MUST NOT/SHALL/SHOULD/SHOULD NOT/MAY as in RFC 2119. Uses two catalogued devices, both new:
[device::NonConvergingTrace](../../_terms.md#devicenonconvergingtrace),
[device::FractionalPartHistogram](../../_terms.md#devicefractionalparthistogram).

## Scope
**In scope:** `memory-bank/visualizations/A000030/viz.html` — the current, only page for this
sequence (no kept drafts) — the `solution.mjs`/`proof.mjs` pair, and
`memory-bank/verify/benford.mjs`, which independently re-checks the page's own embedded statistical
claims about `A008952` and the equidistribution mechanism (a different sequence's numbers, cited on
this page, so the check lives beside the other shared verification scripts rather than inside
`sequences/A000030/`).

**Out of scope:** A008952's own write-up (it is cited, not documented, here); Benford's law as a
general phenomenon beyond the one mechanism (skewed vs. equidistributed fractional parts) this page
demonstrates; the sequence's exact self-similar block structure, which is a restatement of its
definition and carries no picture on this page.

## Architecture

### Overview (prose)
`memory-bank/visualizations/A000030/viz.html` runs Problem (leading-digit frequencies at `N=999`
look flat, 11.1% each) → (1: pushing `N` past a power of ten breaks that flatness and it never
returns, via [device::NonConvergingTrace](../../_terms.md#devicenonconvergingtrace)) → (2: why —
the fractional parts of `log₁₀n` are skewed, via
[device::FractionalPartHistogram](../../_terms.md#devicefractionalparthistogram)) → (3: the
identical rule applied to `A008952` settles onto Benford's law and stays) → (4: why that one
settles — the fractional parts of `n·log₁₀2` are uniform, via the SAME
[device::FractionalPartHistogram](../../_terms.md#devicefractionalparthistogram), reused at the same
scale so "skewed" and "uniform" are directly comparable shapes) → Solution (one rule, two inputs,
two fates, reusing both prior drawings shrunk side by side, stating plainly that A000030 itself does
NOT obey Benford's law under plain counting density).

### Code
`sequences/A000030/solution.mjs` computes `a(n)` by the plain definition (repeated division by 10).
`sequences/A000030/proof.mjs` re-derives every term by an independent log₁₀-based route sharing no
code with the division loop, checks soundness and OEIS agreement, and — since this sequence's real
content is the statistical claim rather than the per-term rule — independently recomputes the
natural-density proportion at six widening `N` and confirms a genuine swing rather than a caption
asserting one. `memory-bank/verify/benford.mjs` separately re-derives, from scratch, the page's
claims about a different sequence (`A008952`'s Benford convergence, checked against an exact
`BigInt` leading-digit computation rather than the page's own fast float-based method) and the
equidistribution mechanism (decile counts of `{n·log₁₀2}` versus `{log₁₀n}` over `n=1..10⁶`).
