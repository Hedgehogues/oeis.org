# A000010 — Euler's totient function

## Vision
`a(n) = phi(n)` counts the positive integers `<= n` coprime to `n`. The naive question ("count
coprimes one by one") gives no handle on why `phi(n)` takes the closed form it does. The page's
central claim: `phi(n) = n * product over distinct prime factors p of n of (1 - 1/p)` — each
distinct prime factor removes exactly a `1/p` fraction of whatever survived the earlier passes, and
that removal is a visible strike over a strip of `1..n`, not an asserted formula.

[device::TotientSieveStrip](../../_terms.md#devicetotientsievestrip).

## Architecture

### Overview (prose)
`memory-bank/visualizations/A000010/viz.html` runs Problem (how many of `1..20` share no factor
with 20, shown as a plain, undifferentiated strip) → (1: the distinct prime factors of 20 are just
2 and 5) → (2: strike every multiple of 2, via
[device::TotientSieveStrip](../../_terms.md#devicetotientsievestrip) — half struck, 10 remain) →
(3: strike every multiple of 5 still standing, the SAME device's second pass — a fifth of the
survivors, 2 more struck, 8 remain) → Solution (8 survivors, the product formula
`20×(1-1/2)×(1-1/5)=8` computed independently, and a verified-match badge citing `proof.mjs`'s
actual run).

### Code
`sequences/A000010/solution.mjs` computes `phi(n)` by the direct definition: count `k` in `1..n`
with `gcd(k,n)=1`. `sequences/A000010/proof.mjs` does not reuse that count: it re-derives `phi(n)`
via trial-division factorization and the product formula (a structurally different computation,
sharing no code with the gcd-counting loop), separately checks the named special case `phi(p)=p-1`
for every prime in range, separately checks multiplicativity `phi(a*b)=phi(a)*phi(b)` on every
actual coprime pair in range, and only then compares final counts to OEIS's published terms.
